An AI agent for the energy market

When a household switches its electricity supplier, companies have 24 hours and a 230-page rulebook. My agent answers questions from that rulebook with a source, calculates the deadlines and checks how far a switch has got.

How it works

The model decides, the tools know the facts

The agent reads a question, picks the tool it needs, checks the result and only then answers. Facts come from tools, never from the model’s memory.

Results

Measured, not guessed

29 test questions with known answers, checked automatically – including whether the agent invents a date that no tool returned.

Challenges

What was hard, and how I solved it

01

AI is bad at dates

Deadlines depend on working days, holidays in every state and even 24 and 31 December. Code calculates them from the official rules; the model only picks the rule and explains it.

  • Rule table
  • Working-day calendar
02

Is the answer right?

29 test questions with known answers, checked on every change. The check also catches answers that restate a correct result wrongly, such as a wrong weekday.

  • Golden questions
  • pytest
03

Local models skipped the tools

They answered from memory. A guardrail now rejects answers without a tool result: 41% → 59% for the weakest model.

  • Guardrail
  • Ollama
04

Same model, different result

On the laptop the same model scores 79% instead of 86–90%. Not a bug but a trade-off between keeping data local and quality – the numbers make it a clear conversation.

  • Mistral (EU)
  • Local models
